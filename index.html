Your Tapo camera, recorded on motion, saved to your own YouTube.

tapo-uploader records a TP-Link Tapo camera only when it detects motion and uploads the footage as private YouTube videos, one playlist per day. No cloud subscription, no firmware hacking, no storage to buy.

How it works

The camera can't upload by itself, so a small always-on program does it from outside. The camera is never modified.

videocam

Camera

Streams over RTSP and announces motion through ONVIF events.

arrow_forward
sensors

Motion

Keeps a short ring buffer: 30 s before the trigger, 5 min after.

arrow_forward
cloud_upload

Upload

Once a day, the clips become one video on your YouTube, OneDrive or cloud.

arrow_forward
playlist_play

Playlist

One playlist or folder per day, event times as chapters. Files are deleted.

Everything you need, nothing you don't

sensors

Motion-triggered

Pre-roll and post-roll are configurable. Late motion extends the window. Person detection counts too.

lock

Private by default

Videos go to your own channel as private. Switch to unlisted or public with one setting.

memory

No re-encoding

ffmpeg -c copy keeps the CPU near zero, so a Raspberry Pi is plenty.

delete_outline

Frees the disk

Each file is deleted right after a successful upload. A few GB of space are enough.

verified_user

Resilient

Restarts ffmpeg when the stream drops, retries uploads, respects the YouTube quota, and never logs your camera password.

schedule

Two modes

Motion mode uploads one video a day. Continuous mode records 6-hour segments.

play_circle

Watch before it is uploaded

Clips still waiting for the daily upload show on your page, marked Not uploaded yet, and play right there.

history

Change storage, lose nothing

Switch from YouTube to OneDrive and the old videos stay on your page. Every video is marked with where it lives.

inventory_2

Ready-made image

A Docker image is published for you on every release (amd64 and arm64): pull it, no build on your server.

New · optional

One server, a few friends

Turn on multi-account mode and several trusted people share the same server. Everyone links their own camera and their own YouTube channel, and sees only their own videos. Leave it off and nothing changes.

groups

Everyone gets a page

Camera status, last motion, last upload and the daily playlists of their own YouTube channel, in one place.

smart_display

Their own storage

Each person picks their own YouTube channel (private videos), their own OneDrive or their own WebDAV, unless you choose a storage you run for everyone.

login

Sign in your way

Local accounts, or Nextcloud: its groups become roles, so administrators and users come from the groups you already have.

router

A guide for the router

Each friend gets step-by-step instructions, or a ready-made prompt for an AI assistant. Ports are opened only for the server's IP.

admin_panel_settings

An admin page

Users, camera status and the day's YouTube quota at a glance. Deactivate someone and their recorder stops at once.

lock

Encrypted and isolated

Passwords and tokens are stored encrypted. Camera addresses are checked so nobody can use the server to reach its own network.

info One Google project's YouTube quota serves about 5 or 6 people at one upload a day; the guide explains how to go beyond that. Read the multi-account guide.

New · optional

Choose where the recordings go

YouTube stays the default. If you would rather keep the footage somewhere else, send it to your own OneDrive, Nextcloud, cloud or disk instead, and change your mind whenever you like. Step-by-step guide to set each one up.

smart_display

YouTube

Private videos, one playlist per day. The default, unchanged.

cloud_queue

OneDrive

Each person connects their own with a Microsoft sign-in. The app only sees its own folder, never the rest of your files.

cloud

WebDAV

Nextcloud, ownCloud, Synology and anything else that speaks WebDAV.

sync_alt

rclone

Mega, S3-compatible storage, Dropbox, Google Drive and dozens more, set by the server administrator (Mega not tested by the author).

folder

A folder

Plain files on the machine that runs it.

swap_horiz

Switch any time

Earlier destinations stay in your list and their videos stay on your page, each marked with where it is. Removing one from the list deletes nothing.

play_circle With several accounts the server picks the default and each person may use their own YouTube, OneDrive or WebDAV where you allow it. Every video has two buttons: Watch here plays it on the page (not for private YouTube videos, which YouTube refuses to embed), Open the video goes to where it lives. Quick start

You need Python 3.10+, ffmpeg, a Google account with a YouTube channel, and the camera account from the Tapo app. The full guide covers every step, including the Google Cloud project and the router.

$ git clone https://github.com/LuigiElleBalotta/tapo-uploader.git
$ cd tapo-uploader && python -m venv .venv && . .venv/bin/activate
$ pip install -r requirements.txt
$ cp .env.example .env              # camera user, password, address
$ python tapo_uploader.py check       # does the camera answer?
$ python tapo_uploader.py auth        # Google login, once
$ python tapo_motion.py run           # record on motion, upload once a day

Questions

Can a Tapo C200 upload to YouTube?

Not by itself. This project adds it from outside: it reads the camera's RTSP stream and uploads with the official YouTube Data API.

Do I need to root or flash the camera?

No. The stock firmware already offers RTSP and ONVIF once you create the Camera Account in the Tapo app.

Will the videos be public?

No. On YouTube they are uploaded as private and only your Google account can see them; on WebDAV, rclone or a folder they sit where only you (and the server's administrator) can read them.

Can I keep them somewhere other than YouTube?

Yes (setup guide): your own OneDrive, WebDAV (Nextcloud and similar), a local folder, or anything rclone supports, such as Mega or S3 storage.

If I change where videos go, do I lose the old ones?

No. The earlier destination stays in your list and its videos stay on your page, each marked with its source. They are never moved or deleted.

Can several people share one server?

Yes, with the optional multi-account mode: each person links their own camera and YouTube channel and sees only their own videos.

Is it a replacement for a cloud plan?

It replaces the storage part with your own YouTube channel. It does not replace the Tapo app's cloud timeline.

verified_user Videos go only where the person who signs in chose: their own YouTube channel by default, or their own OneDrive or cloud. The software is self-hosted and sends nothing to the author. Read the privacy policy.
